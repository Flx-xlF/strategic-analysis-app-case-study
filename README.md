## The Shift: Why Separate Channels Collide in AI Search

Modern corporate communications operates across earned media, owned stories, executive channels, and regional stakeholder briefings—historically assuming that different audiences consume those channels separately.

In the age of zero-click AI search, that separation disappears: answer engines decompose a single question into **5–11 parallel sub-queries**, pulling passages from press coverage, forums, owned pages, and legacy files into one context window at once.

As a result, incoherence becomes the central vulnerability. Discrepancies across central, regional, or cantonal teams dilute the company’s voice when AI models cross-reference them.

---

## The Two-Way Approach & GEO (Without Robotic Copy-Pasting)

Terrain explores how corporate communications can gain traction during this shift across two connected directions:

* **Outside-In:** Audits where narratives come through in media and AI search—and where they lose pull-through or collide across channels.
* **Top-Down:** Enforces centrally defined and administered narratives plus channel-adapted **GEO** standards across both earned media and owned Corporate Stories.

> **What is GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)?**
> Traditional SEO helps web pages rank as clickable links. **GEO** means structuring clear, verifiable facts into your texts (leading with the core conclusion, writing self-contained passages, and keeping core facts consistent) so AI answer engines accurately pick up and quote your message when generating an answer.

**Tailoring to context, not copy-pasting:** Avoiding discrepancies does *not* mean robotically copy-pasting key messages everywhere. It means using AI to analyze the specific channel and audience context and tailoring the central narrative organically so it fits a press inquiry, an owned story, or a regional briefing while keeping the underlying facts consistent.

## Enterprise IT Integration & In-Workflow Delivery

Establishing coherent narratives and frameworks top-down fails if rules sit in static documents inside folders—in a busy environment, teams overlook them in a hurry. Equally, in a regulated enterprise operating across central and regional hubs, this system would not be built as a standalone "shadow IT" silo.

A production setup connects existing enterprise layers across two streams:

1. **Outside-In Layer:** Connects to external feeds—licensed newspaper, media and social aggregators (**Factiva, Meltwater, Talkwalker**) plus public LLM APIs—to ingest ongoing article and answer streams. Because these produce huge amounts of data, the stream is digested into structured data and processed statistically and by LLMs to identify where messages come through and where work needs to be done.
2. **Top-Down Layer:** Central Comms administers key narratives and GEO frameworks, translates them into **prompt recipes**, and streams them through existing company systems right where work is done: via **document editor add-ins (`Word / Docs`), Web CMS browser extensions, and email assistants (`Outlook / Teams`)**. Keeping friction low and delivering immediate daily benefit ensures teams actually use them.

---

## Realistic Limits: AI Frames the Context, Humans Iterate

There is no "one-shot, ready-to-send" output. An LLM will not produce a finished, ready-to-send statement or corporate story in a single pass (at least for the time being).

AI analyzes sensitivities, enforces the GEO framework, and tailors the narratives; **humans bring unwritten context and iterate**. Its real value is handing teams three concrete inputs in one place:
1. **Tailored versions of the company’s key narrative**
2. **A structured GEO framework**
3. **The most important communication risks derived from the Outside-In branch**

…so human communicators can iterate and finalize the text with full context.
